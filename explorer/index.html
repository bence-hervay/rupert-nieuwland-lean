Rupertness and Nieuwland explorer

Bence Hervay

Background

Proving the Non-Rupertness of the Rhombicosidodecahedron was one of my largest self-motivated research projects, taking more than 1000 hours over two years. It needed numerous novel ideas, and I did it with minimal AI assistance (mostly for coding, and for quickly putting the article together once the proof was finished).

Afterwards I had many ideas to go further:

  • generalising my techniques to arbitrary polyhedra
  • proving stronger claims in this topic
  • verifying everything in Lean for higher confidence (although my original attempt relied on exact arithmetic, the theory wasn't formally verified)

Although I had targeted only the rhombicosidodecahedron, many of my techniques turned out to generalise to basically arbitrary convex solids. With significant further work (this time with heavy AI assistance), I developed a system of provably bounded and empirically fast algorithms that answer various questions about Rupertness automatically (or, for some more obscure solids, semi-automatically, with geometric “hints” that make the computation more practical). I put this explorer together to present some of my results visually and interactively!

What this explorer contains

  • A Lean-verified Rupertness result for 155 solids, 133 Rupert and 22 Nopert:
    • Platonic solids (5)
    • Archimedean solids (13)
    • Catalan solids (13)
    • Johnson solids (92)
    • Prisms (Pn) (9)
    • Antiprisms (An) (9)
    • Triakis tetrahedra (Tα) (9)
    • Other (5)
  • A Lean-verified exact Nieuwland number ν for each of these solids.
  • A Lean-verified exact optimal pose (for the Rupert ones) that yields a weak fit after scaling by ν.

All Nieuwland numbers and poses are specified algebraically, by polynomial systems over the rationals with respective rational bounding boxes that contain exactly one root!
